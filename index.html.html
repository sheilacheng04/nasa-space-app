Chapter 3

2What makes it “Super”

Scientists watch the temperature of a patch of the central Pacific called Niño 3.4. +0.5°C above normal marks El Niño. Above +2.0°C is very strong. The old record was +2.6°C. In early November the weekly reading was +3.3°C, and forecasters expect a peak near +3.8°C in December.

Chapter 5

4What it does to people

🔥 Heat

Global temperatures climb. The WMO says 2027 could set a new world heat record.

🏜 Drought and fire

Rain fails over the Philippines, Indonesia, Australia and southern Africa. Crops dry out and fires spread.

🌊 Floods

Heavy rain hits Peru, Ecuador, East Africa and southern Brazil. Rivers spill over.

🌾 Food and health

Failed harvests raise prices. One projection warns of up to 451,000 extra heat deaths worldwide.

Chapter 6

5Where NISAR radar comes in

👁 Sees through clouds

Radar works day and night, even in storms, so it can watch floods and fires as they happen.

💧 Water looks dark

Open water reflects the radar away. Spreading floods and shrinking reservoirs show up clearly.

🌡 It can’t feel heat

Heat comes from ocean and weather data. Radar shows what heat and rain do to land, crops and water.
