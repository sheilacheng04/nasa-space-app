# Project summary

The Two-Faced Pacific starts with a question at Angat reservoir: is its shoreline changing? Real NISAR browse imagery anchors a field journal about land and water during the 2026 El Niño season. Radar observations lead; ocean conditions provide context.

Four study areas connect different questions: water storage at Angat, rice cycles in Central Luzon, a wet-side hypothesis in Peru, and flood-method testing in Dadu. Dadu’s 2022 floods occurred during La Niña and are not evidence for a 2026 El Niño impact.

The app combines verified scene catalogs, dated imagery, acquisition gaps, monthly ERA5 weather, NOAA ocean indices, and source-linked reporting. Each site explains its hypothesis, competing human factors, evidence requirements, and what would weaken the interpretation. Headline sentiment describes language rather than climate risk.

Missing evidence remains visible. Calibrated backscatter, water-area estimates, and independent validation are unavailable until authenticated GCOV processing is completed. Browse images never supply invented measurements. Simulated ocean and globe explanations are labeled.

An accessible site explorer, offline HTML, reproducible scripts, and provenance records make the work inspectable. The author’s Philippine story and photograph remain open slots. NISAR’s repeated observations can support records, but one season cannot prove attribution. The next step is calibrated analysis with validation.
