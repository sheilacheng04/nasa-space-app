# Project summary

The Two-Faced Pacific connects a climate story with local questions about water and land. Built for NASA Space Apps, it explains how El Niño shifts rainfall, then invites people to examine Central Luzon, Angat reservoir, and Dadu through a radar observatory.

The application separates evidence from illustration. A dated NOAA advisory supplies ocean context, while ERA5 reanalysis provides temperature and precipitation histories. Real regional NISAR browse images introduce the study areas without pretending to be quantitative findings. A reproducible pipeline prepares calibrated radar acquisitions for comparisons, polarization exploration, change maps, and accessible time series.

Each site presents a hypothesis, the evidence needed to support it, alternative human drivers, and observations that could disprove it. Reservoir operations and irrigation therefore remain part of the explanation. Users can also inspect linked news, compare sentiment methods, and add field observations through private, browser-based CSV imports.

The open-source project is prepared for Vercel and includes a self-contained demonstration. Sources, dates, processing choices, and missing data stay visible. Quantitative site findings await the team's validated dataset; illustrative controls are labeled accordingly. The goal is to make radar understandable while keeping scientific uncertainty intact, creating a foundation for future flood mapping, crop monitoring, and community storytelling.
