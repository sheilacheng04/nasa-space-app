# Six-slide outline

1. **One ocean, uneven consequences.** Pacific illustration, one dated NOAA observation, and the question: how do climate shifts appear in land and water? Distinguish context from local attribution.
2. **Why radar, why these places.** NISAR sees surface structure and moisture through cloud. Locator maps for Central Luzon, Angat and Dadu. Explain the Philippine connection and the historical flood-test role.
3. **The observation workflow.** ASF discovery → quality/metadata review → common grid → COG/PNG + provenance → viewer. Show actual scene IDs. Explain why L-band and C-band are not directly differenced.
4. **An interactive evidence desk.** Demonstrate swipe, available polarizations, change layer and weather time series. Before the dataset arrives, label this slide as an illustrative interaction demonstration, with the real regional browse shown separately.
5. **A hypothesis that can fail.** Angat rainfall versus releases, or Luzon rainfall versus irrigation/harvest. List the required field evidence and a clear disconfirming observation. Add a genuine team photo when supplied.
6. **Open, reusable, ready for the next observation.** Source register, MIT code, Vercel build and dataset contract. State current limitations. Future work: validated water mapping, crop-cycle analysis, longer baselines and uncertainty estimates.
